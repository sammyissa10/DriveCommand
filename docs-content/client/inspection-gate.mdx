# Turning the pre-trip inspection on or off

<Callout variant="info">
When this is on, a driver cannot start a trip until they have walked the truck and answered every item on your checklist. What they found is kept against the vehicle, so a defect raised on Tuesday morning is still there on Friday when somebody asks.
</Callout>

## Where the settings are

Open **Settings**, then **Operations**. There are two switches on that page and they work together.

### Require a pre-trip inspection

Off by default. Turn it on and, in the app's own words: *drivers complete a full-screen walkaround before a trip can start. An inspection stays valid for 24 hours on the same truck, so a second trip the same day does not ask again.*

<Callout variant="tip">
Twenty-four hours means twenty-four hours — not "today". A driver who inspects at 23:50 is not asked again at ten past midnight. That is on purpose: the alternative punishes the night shift for doing it right.
</Callout>

### Block the trip when a critical item fails

On by default, and it only does anything while the first switch is on.

- **On** — a failed critical item stops the trip and alerts dispatch. An owner or manager can still override it from the trip, with a written reason that stays on the record.
- **Off** — a critical failure is written against the truck, and the driver carries on.

## Deciding which items are critical

Being on the checklist and being able to stop a trip are two different things. An item stops a trip only if it is marked as one that blocks dispatch, which you do when you build the checklist under **Checklists**.

<Callout variant="warning">
If none of your inspection items is marked that way, the Operations page tells you so directly — turning both switches on protects nothing, because there is no item that can fail hard enough to stop anybody. Worth checking before you rely on it.
</Callout>

## Overriding a blocked trip

Sometimes the load has to move and you know the truck is safe — the failed item was a mis-tap, or it has already been fixed on the yard.

<StepFlow>
  <StepFlow.Step title="Open the trip">
    Find it on the Today's Trips report or the Live Board — a blocked trip is sorted to the top of both — and open the trip itself.
  </StepFlow.Step>
  <StepFlow.Step title="Find the Pre-trip inspection section">
    It sits near the top of the trip, under the driver and truck. It tells you where the inspection stands, and what failed if something did.
  </StepFlow.Step>
  <StepFlow.Step title="Press Override inspection">
    Only an owner or a manager sees this. It says what it does: it lets this trip start without a passing inspection.
  </StepFlow.Step>
  <StepFlow.Step title="Write the reason">
    A reason is required, and a one-word reason will not be accepted. Your name, your reason and the time are kept on the trip permanently — for example *Mechanic replaced the marker lamp on site at 05:10*.
  </StepFlow.Step>
  <StepFlow.Step title="The driver can start">
    The failed item stays on the record. The override sits beside it, not instead of it.
  </StepFlow.Step>
</StepFlow>

## Who this affects

| Person | What changes |
|---|---|
| Drivers | They get a walkaround to complete before the trip will start |
| Dispatchers and managers | They are alerted when a trip is blocked, and can override with a reason |
| Owners | Same as managers, plus the two switches |

## Turning it off again

Switching **Require a pre-trip inspection** off stops any new trip asking for one. It does not delete what has already been recorded — inspections that were done, and defects that were found, stay on the record.
